Why the surrounding neighborhood matters

California Air Resources Board links warehouse growth to concentrated truck activity, congestion, and air pollution, and records community concerns about noise, safety, and infrastructure. It also identifies unequal patterns of development as an environmental justice concern.

In its 2026 Region in Crisis report, the Center for Community Action and Environmental Justice reports housing demolitions and residential-to-industrial rezoning in the Inland Empire. This is the community organization's regional assessment; individual sites need their own evidence.

CARB's San Bernardino / Muscoy community profile identifies freight activity, cumulative pollution burden, and nearby sensitive locations, including schools and daycare facilities.

What this map shows

In Clusters mode, the selected category appears only as circles. In Footprints mode, it appears only as building shapes. Other categories provide footprint context at neighborhood scales. Mint identifies tagged residential buildings; neutral shapes include other and unspecified types. The same selected buildings are never drawn in both layers. Current proximity can help identify places to investigate, but it does not establish demolition, encroachment over time, truck routes, exposure, or health impacts. Those questions require dated land-use, permitting, traffic, or monitoring records.

The opening view highlights footprints explicitly tagged as warehouses in OpenStreetMap. Other industrial buildings are a separate category; unspecified buildings are never assumed to be warehouses.

Nearby building centroids become clusters. Their colors represent the most common building category; ties follow legend order. Choose Footprints to see individual building shapes, or filter by type to discover a different pattern.

Read the map honestly

Counts describe mapped buildings, not people, homes, floor area, or a complete building census. Cluster reach uses screen pixels, so groups change with zoom. Unknown means no specific building type. OSM's building tag describes architectural type and may differ from current use. Minor types are grouped as Other.

Zooming out now stops at level 10. Warehouse clusters load at regional scales up to 1:600,000. Surrounding footprints, other building types, and Footprints mode load at 1:80,000 and closer to control the amount of detailed geometry requested. Use place search to travel anywhere in the US, or choose an Inland Empire district from the controls panel. Explore all 50 US states where OSM footprints exist; completeness varies. The source covers North America, so neighboring countries may also appear. Category counts describe all mapped buildings in view; the headline describes the selected category. Counts are server queries for buildings intersecting the map extent; cluster membership is based on centroids.
