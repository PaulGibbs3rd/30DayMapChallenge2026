# Warehouse Country — research and map interpretation
Sources reviewed November 4, 2026.

## Concerns supported by the sources
- **Freight activity and neighborhood conditions.** The California Air Resources Board's 2026 research solicitation describes concentrated truck activity, congestion and air pollution associated with warehouse growth; it also records concerns about noise, safety, infrastructure and economic effects. It identifies disparate development patterns as an environmental justice concern and notes important gaps in estimating site-specific activity. [CARB: Community-driven assessment](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/documents/community-driven-assessment-warehouse-activities-and-impacts).
- **Housing and land-use change.** The Center for Community Action and Environmental Justice's 2026 Region in Crisis II assessment reports home demolitions and residential-to-industrial rezoning in the Inland Empire. These are attributed findings of a community advocacy organization, not measurements calculated by this map. [CCAEJ: Region in Crisis 2026](https://www.ccaej.org/region-in-crisis-2026).
- **Cumulative burdens near sensitive locations.** CARB's San Bernardino / Muscoy community profile identifies freight-related sources, high cumulative pollution burden and nearby schools and daycare facilities. The profile reflects that community and its planning history; it does not establish a modeled exposure value for every building in this map. [CARB: San Bernardino / Muscoy](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/capp/com/cip/san-bernardino-muscoy).
- **Policy response.** South Coast AQMD describes WAIRE as an indirect-source program designed to reduce goods-movement air pollution from warehouse facilities. The map does not determine which sites meet the rule's applicability criteria. [South Coast AQMD: WAIRE](https://www.aqmd.gov/home/rules-compliance/compliance/waire-program).

## Consequences for the map
Keep the surrounding building fabric visible. Filter the focus layer's clusters independently of the background. Draw actual warehouse footprints in amber and explicitly residential footprints in mint. Keep other and unknown types neutral instead of inferring homes from shape or proximity. Sidebar category counts describe the whole mapped extent, while the large number describes the selected category.

This OSM snapshot illustrates current spatial proximity. Demonstrating encroachment or displacement over time requires dated building/warehouse records, demolition and development permits, zoning histories, or historical imagery. Measuring pollution and impacts requires additional traffic, monitoring and exposure evidence. The map does not draw arbitrary impact buffers or label nearby buildings as harmed.
