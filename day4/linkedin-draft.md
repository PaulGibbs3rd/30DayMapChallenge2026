What does a warehouse landscape look like when you step back?

For Day 4 of #30DayMapChallenge, I turned OpenStreetMap building footprints into an interactive map of warehouse concentrations in California's Inland Empire.

The map opens near Redlands. Amber warehouses sit among mint residential buildings and neutral other footprints. Clusters highlight the selected category while the surrounding neighborhood stays visible.

I kept warehouses separate from other industrial buildings—and left unspecified building types as unknown. This is a view of what OSM records, rather than a complete warehouse inventory.

CARB documents concerns about truck activity, congestion, air pollution, noise and safety around warehouse growth. CCAEJ's 2026 regional assessment also reports housing demolition and residential-to-industrial rezoning. The map invites a closer look at these spatial relationships; its current footprints do not establish expansion over time or measure exposure.

Try the map, then explore a place you know. Where do warehouses meet everyday neighborhood life?

Built with @arcgis/create, ArcGIS Maps SDK for JavaScript, and Calcite Design System.
