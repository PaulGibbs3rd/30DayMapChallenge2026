A city’s edge is a patchwork of people, policy, and working landscapes.

Compare two places in Seattle and King County at the same map scale. Pan each map independently, zoom either one to change both scales, and click either map to sample a different point. The population figures describe the surrounding Census tracts, not everything visible in the map windows.

People · a 2020 snapshot

494 King County Census tracts, joined to total population from the 2020 Census redistricting data. Density = total population ÷ original Census land area in square miles. Cards describe the entire surrounding tract, not a city, neighborhood, or farm. Large rural tracts can mix settlements with extensive uninhabited land.

Growth boundary · a planning designation

The county’s urban growth area guides where urban growth is accommodated. “Urban growth area” and “Outside urban growth area” here come from King County’s mapped planning designations, not Census urban/rural classification. Outside the county or without coverage, the map reports unavailable. Boundaries are generalized for display and are not parcel-level determinations.

King County growth areas ↗ · Adopted comprehensive plan ↗

Working land · designated agricultural districts

Gold areas show the county’s five named Agricultural Production Districts. They indicate land designated primarily for agriculture, not a live inventory of active farms. The Snoqualmie Valley’s Fish, Farm & Flood effort balances agriculture, salmon habitat, and flood risk.

Agricultural districts ↗ · Fish, Farm & Flood ↗

Living lands

These are communities and working landscapes, as well as ancestral lands. The Snoqualmie Tribe offers its own interpretation of the valley and its continuing connection to these places. Explore the Tribe’s Ancestral Lands Movement ↗. Tribal map artwork and place-name data have not been reproduced here.

Dates, coverage, and credits

Population: 2020 Census. Planning layers: King County snapshots retrieved November 9, 2026 (Pacific time); the catalog documents 2025 updates. These layers describe different dates and do not demonstrate historical change. Countywide data is included; guided places focus on Seattle and the Snoqualmie Valley. Missing values are not zero. Water-clipped and generalized display polygons are never used to recalculate Census land area.
