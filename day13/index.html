The Desert Feeds the Forest

In April 2014, winds carried Saharan dust across the Atlantic and into the Amazon. Follow its path from desert to rainforest.

Orange shows airborne particles. Brighter orange means higher aerosol optical depth, a measure of how much light dust, smoke, and other particles scatter or absorb.

Moving lines show wind direction. Drag the lens across the orange band and the air around it to compare local winds while keeping the plume in view.

On April 5, satellite lidar found dust several kilometers above the Atlantic. Watch the wind move west over the orange band. Drag the lens north to see where the flow turns east.

The plume reached northeastern South America on April 6. Drag the lens toward the Guianas and watch for winds blowing toward land. The study found that a rain band could also wash dust out before it reached the forest.

By April 8 the plume had reached the Amazon Tall Tower Observatory (ATTO). On April 10, look for winds blowing southwest toward the tower. The station detected a dust pulse even where the satellite map has gaps. Over many crossings, some of that dust settles and delivers phosphorus to Amazon soils.
