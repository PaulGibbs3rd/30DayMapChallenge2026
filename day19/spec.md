# Day 19 · Follow Your Nose

**Working spec · Senses: Smell · November 19, 2026**

Take a self-guided walk from downtown Seattle through Pike Place Market and along Elliott Bay, then head inland through Pioneer Square to the Chinatown–International District (CID). Pike Place’s flower tables anchor the market stretch. Public reviews and firsthand accounts supply clues about what people have smelled while walking these streets. [Official challenge](https://30daymapchallenge.com/2026/) · [Kate McLean’s smellwalking research](https://researchonline.rca.ac.uk/3945/)

## The walk · Westlake to Little Saigon

Start near **Westlake Station at 4th Avenue and Pike Street**. Head downhill on Pike Street toward the Public Market Center clock, then turn onto Pike Place. The mapped route is about **2.6 miles** one way. Allow **60–75 minutes** with brief stops, before any time spent in shops. This is a walking-time estimate. All stops are on public sidewalks, in the covered market aisle, or on the waterfront. [Westlake Station](https://www.soundtransit.org/ride-with-us/stops-stations/westlake-station) · [Pike Place Market map](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/map/)

1. **Mee Sum Pastry · baked buns.** Pause on the Pike Place sidewalk by the Triangle Building’s walk-up counter. A Google reviewer described smelling the baked buns while walking past, and a September 2025 reviewer noticed baking aromas on approach. Use the shop sign as the wayfinding cue. The bakery lists 1526 Pike Place and the market directory lists 1530 in the same building. [Passerby review](https://app.wanderlog.com/list/geoCategory/69642/#:~:text=This%20place%20smells%20amazing%20as%20you%20walk%20by) · [September 2025 review](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/441995/mee-sum-pastry#:~:text=From%20the%20moment%20I%20walked%20up) · [Bakery address](https://www.meesum.com/) · [Market directory](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/vendor/mee-sum-pastry/)
2. **Piroshky Piroshky · cinnamon pastry.** Continue north on Pike Place to the sidewalk at **1908 Pike Place**. A writer walking the market described catching cinnamon in the air as they reached the bakery. Pause along the storefront where there’s space to let people pass. [Firsthand market write-up](https://weekenddistractions.wordpress.com/2008/10/11/a-seattle-tradition/) · [Market directory](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/vendor/piroshky-piroshky/)
3. **Pike Place flower tables · bouquets and greenery.** Cross Pike Place and go into the covered **North Arcade near Stewart Street**. Look for the flower tables around Yang Farm, where the market lists fresh and dried bouquets and seasonal wreaths. A food-tour writer recalled smelling bouquets on the north end of the market. In November, look for any fresh stems on display, dried bouquets, or eucalyptus if available. The stop is the public aisle beside the tables. [Firsthand food-tour account](https://spoonuniversity.com/school/wisconsin/sampled-pike-place-market/) · [Yang Farm](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/vendor/yang-farm/) · [Market guide to fall flowers](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/13-pike-place-market-shops-to-get-into-the-spirit-of-fall/)
4. **Pier 62 · Elliott Bay air.** Follow signs for **MarketFront and Overlook Walk**. Descend to the waterfront promenade using the stairs or Ocean Pavilion elevator, then head north toward Pier 62. Pause on the sidewalk by the pier’s north edge. Feliks Banel’s 2022 account locates a distinctive marine smell at that spot. [Market-to-waterfront directions](https://www.pikeplacemarket.org/ufaq/can-i-walk-to-the-seattle-waterfront/) · [Overlook Walk and elevator](https://waterfrontparkseattle.org/overlook-walk/) · [Firsthand waterfront account](https://mynorthwest.com/history/making-scents-of-how-seattle-smelled-a-century-ago/3577305)

From **Pier 62**, follow the waterfront promenade south past the aquarium, Pier 54, and Colman Dock. Cross Alaskan Way at **South Main Street** and follow Main east into Pioneer Square. Turn right on 2nd Avenue South, then left onto **South Jackson Street** and follow it east to 5th Avenue South. Turn right to the **Historic Chinatown Gate** at South King Street and continue east to 6th Avenue South. [Waterfront promenade](https://waterfrontparkseattle.org/the-park/) · [Pioneer Square walking connections](https://sdotblog.seattle.gov/2024/12/04/pioneer-square-improvements-support-a-more-accessible-and-vibrant-neighborhood/) · [S Jackson Street connection](https://www.seattle.gov/transportation/projects-and-programs/safety-first/vision-zero/projects/s-jackson-st-vision-zero-safety-corridor) · [Chinatown Gate](https://historylink.tours/stop/chinatown-gate/)

5. **Yummy House Bakery · cake aromas.** From South King Street, turn south on 6th Avenue South to **522 6th Avenue South**. Pause by the bakery entrance. In a December 2019 essay, former neighborhood resident Janet Brown described the smell of its cakes drifting down the street. [Janet Brown’s neighborhood account](https://www.asiabythebook.com/voa?offset=1580664929370#:~:text=Floating%20through%20it%20all) · [Bakery address](https://www.yummyhousebakery.com/)
6. **Asia Bar-B-Que · roast pork.** Head back to South King Street and pass **Hing Hay Park**, then turn north at Maynard Avenue South and continue to South Jackson Street. Pause outside **655A South Jackson Street**. A customer described the barbecue aroma drawing attention to the shop, and Brown separately recalled roast pork in the neighborhood air. [Customer review](https://unilocal.fr/etats-unis/seattle/asia-bar-b-que#:~:text=the%20wonderful%20smell%20of%20Asian%20BBQ) · [Janet Brown’s neighborhood account](https://www.asiabythebook.com/voa?offset=1580664929370#:~:text=Floating%20through%20it%20all) · [Restaurant address](https://www.asiabarbqueseattle.com/)
7. **Saigon Deli · food cooking in Little Saigon.** Continue east on South Jackson Street under I‑5 to **1237 South Jackson Street**, just past 12th Avenue South. Pause by the Jackson Street storefront. A customer recalled smelling food cooking as they parked. The review does not identify the dish. [Customer review](https://restaurantjump.com/saigon-deli-98144/#:~:text=could%20smell%20the%20food%20cooking%20as%20we%20parked) · [Seattle’s site record](https://web.seattle.gov/DPD/HistoricalSite/QueryResult.aspx?ID=2147010642)

**Historical scent near 8th and King.** KNKX reported smelling sugar and almonds at the corner during the era of Tsue Chong’s neighborhood fortune-cookie production. Keep it as an optional historical note off South Jackson Street. The company still lists a retail address nearby, while the former factory has new occupants. [KNKX firsthand report](https://www.knkx.org/other-news/2020-02-01/oodles-of-noodles-and-cookies-explore-the-international-district-factory-where-carbs-are-born) · [Tsue Chong retail address](https://tsuechong.com/contact) · [Former factory today](https://www.visitseattle.org/press/press-releases/communitypartnership2025/)

For Link light rail back toward Westlake, return west on South Jackson Street to **International District/Chinatown Station** at 5th Avenue South. [Sound Transit station](https://www.soundtransit.org/ride-with-us/stops-stations/international-district-chinatown-station)

## Evidence and walk notes

- For a food stop, use a public firsthand account that puts the smell outdoors or in the public market aisle. Record the source link, publication date when available, setting, and a short paraphrase. Link to a location reference for the public stopping point. Yelp required device verification during research on November 19, 2026. The CID reports come from sources identified at each stop.
- Pin each stop at the suggested public standing point. Treat review locations as approximate, even if the reviewer estimates how far the aroma traveled. Use equal-sized markers. The reports describe what someone noticed during their visit, and scent may vary with what’s baking, the season, and conditions along the route.
- Make firsthand notes an optional part of the walk. Pause for about 30 seconds and write what you notice, including **None detected** if appropriate. A later field check can record the date, time, and perceived strength for that specific visit.

## Map and interaction

- Open on the whole route from Westlake to Saigon Deli. Draw a continuous walking line and label Pike Place, Overlook Walk, the waterfront promenade, South Main Street, the Historic Chinatown Gate, and South Jackson Street. Mark the covered market segment and the descent to the waterfront. Give the flower stop a distinctive symbol with a text label. Use Pier 62 as the transition between the market and CID sections of the itinerary.
- Selecting a pin or stop in the list opens its walking direction, a **What someone smelled** note, and a source link. Use **Previous stop** and **Next stop** for keyboard and touch. Keep the stop list readable on a phone and include the elevator option in the route directions.
