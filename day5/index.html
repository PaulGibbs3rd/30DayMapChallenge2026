DAY 05 / SENSES: SIGHT

Which places can you see from the Space Needle’s observation deck?

How the analysis works

Each ray starts on an approximate 21 m deck rim facing its target, at 520 ft (158.496 m) above sampled terrain plus 1.5 m eye height. This approximates walking around the deck and avoids starting inside the tower. Targets include illustrative public-space points and a Mount Rainier summit-area sample, initially 2 m above terrain; they do not represent whole landmarks.

ArcGIS tests terrain and loaded 3D buildings. Green marks an unobstructed segment; red begins at the first obstruction. Pending means a result is unavailable, often because a point is outside the current view. City overview covers Seattle. Select Mount Rainier to frame the regional sightline; return to City overview for city targets.

Mount Rainier’s sample uses the USGS GNIS high-point coordinates and sampled terrain elevation. Regional results depend on terrain detail and do not account for clouds or haze.

A model of a view

The City of Seattle’s building shells were derived from 2015–16 lidar and footprints. Buildings added since then, trees, glass, weather and atmospheric visibility are not represented. Results can change with scene detail and camera position. These are exploratory results, not a present-day survey.
